Applying to more jobs does not always mean making more progress. Sanjana Alapati makes that point in this short video about the Reallocation Engine, a tool built for international students on F1 OPT who are looking for work after graduation. Because their unemployment time is limited, the video argues that effort should go to opportunities that are actually worth pursuing.

## Why time matters on OPT

For students on OPT, the days they can spend without a job are limited. The video treats that constraint as the reason a more focused search matters. Sending applications at random uses up time that cannot be recovered, so the engine is designed to give students a more deliberate way to judge which openings deserve their effort.

## What the engine looks at

Instead of relying only on guesswork, the Reallocation Engine evaluates several signals about each opportunity:

- sponsorship history
- whether the job is still active
- role quality
- resume fit
- whether the opportunity works with the student's OPT timeline

These signals are combined into an overall recommendation rather than being read one at a time.

## Some factors outweigh others

The signals are not weighted equally. The video explains that issues like an inactive job posting or a timeline that does not work can outweigh otherwise positive signals. A role with a strong fit still has little value if the posting is no longer active or the dates do not line up with the student's OPT window, so those problems pull the recommendation down.

## Apply, Consider or Skip

Based on the available evidence, each opportunity is grouped into one of three tiers: Apply, Consider or Skip. The purpose of the tiers is to help students concentrate their effort where it matters most, instead of treating every posting as equally deserving of an application.

## Why human judgment stays in charge

The video is clear that the engine does not replace human judgment. Public data cannot capture every detail or recent change, so a recommendation is there to support the student, and the final decision remains theirs. Sanjana's main takeaway is to use the evidence to focus your effort while keeping the decision in your own hands.
