Most weekly updates read like a highlight reel, with the wins dressed up and the unfinished work blended in. In this video, Agrima, a fellow at Humanitarians AI, shows a more honest alternative. She uses Claude to write a small script that logs her week and then revises it so that what is actually finished stays apart from what has only just started.

## Why weekly updates drift toward highlight reels

The video opens with the problem. A typical weekly update tends to present everything in the most flattering way. Agrima wanted something closer to a real log: what actually got done, kept separate from what is just getting started. That framing sets up the rest of the demonstration, which is about structure rather than wording.

## Starting simple with a flat list

The first step is deliberately basic. She asks Claude for a script that simply logs the week, with one flat list and nothing fancy. The week has three items: an article published on Substack, four brutalist videos she produced, and an upcoming Suffolk University lecture with a presentation being built for it. Claude writes a flat list with three entries, no ranking and no spin. Running the script prints the week: the Substack article titled for the rise of faceless AI accounts, the four videos, and the lecture preparation.

## The flaw in a flat list

The video then points out what is wrong with that output. All three entries carry the same weight. A flat list treats "I published an article" the same as "I am still building slides for a lecture that has not happened yet." The items are accurate individually, but together they blur the difference between work that shipped and work that is still ahead. A reader skimming the list could easily come away thinking all three are finished.

## Revising the script to split done from next

The fix is a small revision. Agrima asks Claude to split what is actually finished from what is just starting, using the same week and the same three items but organized into two lists. Under "done" go the article and the four videos. Under "next" go the Suffolk University lecture and the presentation that is still coming together. When the script is run again, the shape of the output changes completely.

## What the revised log says

The new output reports that the Substack article is published and that all four videos are finished in both formats. It then lists the Suffolk University lecture, with meetings underway, and the presentation still being built, under starting next. The narration makes the point plainly: nothing in the second list is finished yet, and saying so is the whole purpose. The result is one article shipped, four videos produced, and one lecture still ahead.

## Key takeaways

- A flat weekly list gives finished work and future plans the same weight.
- Asking for a plain, unranked log first makes the problem visible before you fix it.
- Splitting the log into "done" and "starting next" changes what the reader understands about the week.
- Items that are still being built, such as slides for an upcoming lecture, belong in the "next" list.
- The difference between a recap and a highlight reel is a matter of structure, not tone.
- Revising a script with Claude is a quick way to test a different structure on the same content.

## Try it yourself

Use the same prompt with your own week. Ask Claude for a recap script of what you did, then ask it to split the entries into what is done and what you are starting next. Compare the two outputs, and notice how much more honestly the second version describes what actually happened versus what you have only just started thinking about.
