Many people assume an AI-narrated video is a single step: write a script, generate a voice, and you are done. Aujaswi Agarwal uses this short video to explain why that picture is incomplete. Drawing on a real production, the video lays out how much sits between a rough first pass and a video that is ready to publish, and what that means for anyone planning one.

## The myth of the one-step video

The video begins with the common belief. Most people, it says, think an AI-narrated video is one step. You write a script, generate a voice, and the work is finished. The speaker's response is direct: the real pipeline is a lot bigger than that. The rest of the video describes what the larger pipeline actually contained in this project.

## Rewrites and a full re-record

Getting from a rough first pass to a genuinely publishable video required full script rewrites. That has a knock-on effect that is easy to overlook. Once the wording of the script changed, the narration had to be recorded again in full. Because the voice is generated from the text, a changed script is not a small patch to existing audio. It means a complete re-record, and any plan that assumes a single voiceover pass will miss this cost.

## Layered audio and outside feedback

The work did not stop at the voice. The video describes adding layered audio on top of the narration, and doing multiple rounds of effects work. Those rounds were based on real feedback from other people, not just the creator's own read of the result. The distinction matters: a creator reviewing their own work tends to miss things that fresh listeners catch, and acting on that outside feedback added further passes to the pipeline.

## The gap between draft and polished

The video summarizes the situation by saying the real pipeline can be laid out step by step, and that the gap between a rough draft and a polished final video is bigger than it looks. The point is not that the process is impossible, only that its length is underestimated when people picture it as a single generation step. The final section turns that observation into planning advice.

## Budgeting for revisions

The practical takeaway is about budgeting. Time and resources should be set aside for rewrites and re-recording, not just one voiceover pass. ## Try it yourself

If you are planning an AI-narrated video, write your schedule as if the first voiceover will be thrown away. Add explicit time for a script rewrite, a full re-record, an audio layering pass and at least one round of effects work based on feedback from someone other than yourself. Then compare that plan with a single-pass estimate to see how much the budget changes.
