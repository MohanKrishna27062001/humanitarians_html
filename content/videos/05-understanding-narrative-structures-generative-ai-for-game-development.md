This video is a companion to the fifth chapter of Generative AI for Game Development by Shesh Narayan Gupta. It pushes back on the idea that game stories are just cutscenes between the action. They are architectures, the video says, structures that decide who gets to choose and when. It walks through the main structures with examples from well-known games and explains how generative AI works inside each one.

## Linear and non-linear stories

A linear story is one road that every player walks in the same order. The writer controls tone, pacing and character, and every emotional beat lands where it was placed. God of War is the video's example, a novel you play. Linear is also the easiest shape for AI to follow, since it can generate cutscenes, quests and plot beats without drifting.

Non-linear stories hand the player the pieces out of order. The player explores, investigates and remembers, and the story reassembles itself in their head. Exploration games and detective stories suit this shape, and the joy is discovery and recontextualization, when an old scene suddenly means something new.

## Branching narratives and foldbacks

Once the player has a choice, the story branches. A decision tree maps every choice to its consequence, some shallow and some wide enough for a different life. Some games fake the sprawl with foldbacks, where branches split and then funnel back to one point, giving the appearance of choice at a fraction of the cost. The video cites The Witcher 3, where a spared enemy reshapes the ending hours later, and Mass Effect, which mixes deep side branches with one steady spine.

## Modular, episodic and nested stories

Modular stories ship in pieces: quests taken in any order, each self-contained. Skyrim and Cyberpunk let players build their own evening from missions. Episodic games release like television, and Life Is Strange told its story in five chapters, each complete. Nested stories sit inside other stories, and BioShock Infinite starts as a rescue and ends as a reckoning with fate, with each layer reframing the one above.

## Emergent stories and blends

Emergent stories are never written. Systems collide, such as armies, alliances and accidents, and Mount & Blade players walk away with a war story nobody scripted. Most modern games blend structures. Red Dead Redemption 2 runs a linear spine, hangs modular missions off it, and lets emergent moments interrupt. The video frames the whole set as one trade: agency against control. Linear gives the author everything, emergent gives the player everything, and the rest negotiate the middle.

## Two philosophies of authorship

AI Dungeon threw out the script entirely, so the story improvises around whatever the player types, which is emergent narrative on a language model. Bandersnatch went the other way as a hand-built branching film with five endings. The video says AI Dungeon co-authors with you in real time, while Bandersnatch performs authorship and lets you steer. Generative AI works inside these structures: linear keeps it coherent, branching multiplies its variations, and emergent sets it loose.

## Key takeaways

- Game stories are structures that decide who gets to choose and when.
- Linear stories trade freedom for control; non-linear stories trade control for discovery.
- Branching stories are decision trees, and foldbacks keep the writing budget manageable.
- Modular and episodic designs release stories in playable pieces.
- Emergent stories come from systems, not scripts.
- Most games blend structures, and each choice trades agency against control.
- Structure shapes emotion and meaning, and it decides what the player believes their choices were worth.
