Someone hears "legal memo" and assumes it means a final legal opinion, Claude's confident answer on their case. The video says that is the wrong word. A memo from Claude is a working draft analysis, not a verified opinion, and the gap between those two is where a quietly dead citation can hide.

## Memo versus opinion

The opening question is whether Claude can give a legal opinion on your case. The video's answer is that what it produces is a draft analysis of the law as it stands, and that status does not change until every citation in it has been checked. It is tempting to treat a confident, well-cited memo as ready to act on, with every case named and every rule stated plainly.

## The citation that reads like all the others

The video imagines one citation that names a real case correctly, by name and by holding, except that the case was overruled last year. A memo that reads confidently carries that dead case forward as if it still controlled the outcome. Nothing on the page signals the problem; it looks exactly like every correct citation around it. This is the invisible failure the episode is about.

## What a working handoff looks like

A memo handoff that works has three parts:

- The legal question and the facts go in.
- A structured analysis comes out: issue, rule, application, conclusion.
- A required verification step happens before anyone relies on the result.

In the video's illustration, the same memo comes back with one citation stamped in the margin as not confirmed and flagged to check. That flagged citation is checked against the law as it stands today. Only once every citation in the memo is confirmed does it move from draft analysis to something an attorney can actually rely on.

## Two cautions

The first caution is that an unflagged citation is not automatically confirmed. No flag does not mean checked; it means someone still has to look. The second is that a memo coming back heavily corrected at the verification step is not a failure. Catching a wrong or outdated citation there is the verification step doing its job.

- A legal memo from Claude is a working draft analysis, not a verified legal opinion.
