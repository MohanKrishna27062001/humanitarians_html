The AI in project tools can now write your status report in one click, producing a clean paragraph you could send to your boss or client. Sanjana, a project manager at Humanitarians AI, points out that a status report has your name on it. The question is not whether the AI can write it, but whether you can trust it before you hit send.

## The report is a draft, not the status

The central idea is that an AI status report is built only from what is on your board, and a confident paragraph can hide an empty or stale board. The summary button produces genuinely good writing, which is exactly the trap. As a time-saver it is a gift; as the truth it is a liability. Your job is to verify it before you sign it.

The video makes a specific point about the color green. The AI marks status green by counting closed tickets, but green is a judgment about whether you will actually make it, not a fact. The AI cannot see a vendor who has gone quiet, an engineer who is out, or a call that moved the deadline.

## Three checks: sourced, current, complete

1. Sourced. Every claim should point to something real on the board, such as a closed ticket or an actual date, not smooth prose filling a gap.
2. Current. A status is only as true as the board behind it, and the AI will happily summarize a board nobody has touched in two weeks.
3. Complete. The most important line is usually the one not logged anywhere, such as a blocker in a Slack thread or a risk from a call.

If all three pass, you sign. If any fails, you fix it first.

## A real sprint: the verification pass

In the first demo, the AI is asked only to pull what is on the board into a clean update, not to decide whether the team is on track and not to invent anything. The draft reports ten tickets closed and two in progress. Every line matches a real ticket, and the board was updated that morning. The report is almost complete, and the missing piece is something only the human can add: the export feature depends on a vendor API that has been flaky all week, and that appears on no card. One added line flags it as a risk, and now the report is sourced, current and complete.

## The green trap

In the second demo the AI writes that the project is on track and all green. It fails all three checks. On track is the AI's guess from counting tickets. Nobody has touched the board in ten days because the team quietly moved to a hot fix, so the AI is summarizing a stale board. And the real blocker, a key engineer out sick, appears nowhere. Forwarding that would tell a client a confident lie.

## Key takeaways

- A one-click AI status report is a draft, not the status itself.
- Green is a judgment about whether you will make it, not a count of closed tickets.
- Check that every claim is sourced to a real ticket or date.
- Check that the board is current before trusting any summary of it.
- Add what the AI cannot see: vendor problems, absences, deadline changes, risks from calls.
- The AI writes the paragraph; you write the one line that actually matters.
- Your signature means you ran all three checks.

## Try it yourself

Next time your tool offers to write your status, ask the AI to draft the update with every claim tagged to the exact ticket or data it came from, and to separately list everything it could not verify from the board alone. Read that second list closely, since it is your verification checklist. A confident paragraph with no sources is the one never to forward.
