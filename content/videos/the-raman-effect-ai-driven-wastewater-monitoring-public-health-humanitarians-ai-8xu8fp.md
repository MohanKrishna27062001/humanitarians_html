The Raman Effect project, run by Humanitarians AI, sets out to change how public health surveillance works by pairing a laboratory technique with machine learning. ## What wastewater-based epidemiology is

The video describes wastewater-based epidemiology, or WBE, as a powerful tool for monitoring public health. Instead of testing people one at a time, it analyzes pooled wastewater for pathogens, pollutants and other substances. Because the sample reflects a whole community, it gives a broad view of what is present in a population.

## Adding surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy

The project aims to enhance this approach with surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy, known as SERS. SERS uses the Raman effect, and the video says it offers non-destructive, highly sensitive detection of chemical structures at low concentrations. Those two qualities matter for wastewater, where the substances of interest may be present only in small amounts, and where a method that does not destroy the sample is useful.

## Where machine learning comes in

Raman measurements produce complex spectra, and reading them well is difficult. The project plans to integrate advanced machine learning algorithms, in particular deep learning and generative AI, to interpret that spectral data. The video claims this can bring unprecedented accuracy to the interpretation, and it presents the AI analysis as the part that turns raw measurements into useful detection and quantification.

## What the system is meant to detect

According to the video, the AI-powered analysis is intended to improve both the detection and the quantification of various substances. It names emerging pathogens and pollutants as examples. The aim is a monitoring approach that gives a clearer picture of what is in a community's wastewater, including threats that are new.

## The vision: real-time, affordable monitoring

The video closes on a picture of real-time, cost-effective monitoring that sets new standards in public health surveillance. It describes the project as developing sophisticated AI software to analyze the data, supported by lab work and chemistry expertise. The stated ambition is to transform public health monitoring globally, so the work spans both the software and the laboratory side.
