A job can look promising at first glance, but one positive factor does not always make it a strong opportunity. In this video, Sanjana Alapati explains how the reallocation engine evaluates job opportunities by combining several decision signals, so that students can decide where to focus their effort.

## Why one signal is not enough

The starting point is that a role that looks good on a single measure can still be a poor use of time. The engine therefore looks at several signals together. Each signal covers a different way an opportunity can succeed or fail, and the combined picture is what drives the recommendation.

## Sponsorship history

The first signal is sponsorship history. It looks at whether a company has previously sponsored or hired international talent. This can provide useful evidence, but the video is careful about its limits: past sponsorship does not guarantee future sponsorship. It is a clue about a company's habits, not a promise.

## Job liveness

Another signal is job liveness. A role may look like a good fit, but if the posting is old, duplicated, inactive or no longer accepting applications, it may not be worth prioritizing. Checking whether a posting is still alive keeps effort away from roles that no longer exist in practice.

## Resume fit and role quality

The engine also considers resume fit and role quality, which are two separate questions. Resume fit looks at how closely a candidate's skills and experience match the job requirements. Role quality looks at whether the opportunity is relevant, credible and aligned with the candidate's goals. A role can match your skills and still be a weak opportunity, or the reverse.

## OPT timing

For students on OPT, timing matters. Even a strong opportunity may become less useful if the hiring timeline or start date does not work with the student's OPT situation. The video notes that in some cases timeline concerns can outweigh otherwise positive signals.

## Signals work together

These signals are not applied one at a time. The video gives an example: a role might have strong resume fit, but if the posting is inactive and the sponsorship evidence is weak, the overall recommendation may still be lower. Opportunities can then be grouped into apply, consider or skip.

## Support, not replacement

The engine is designed to support judgment, not replace it. Public data can be incomplete or outdated, and company policies can change. The stated goal is to use multiple signals to focus your effort on stronger opportunities while keeping the final decision in your hands.

## Key takeaways

- One positive factor does not make a job a strong opportunity.
- Sponsorship history shows whether a company has sponsored or hired international talent, but does not guarantee future sponsorship.
- Job liveness checks whether a posting is old, duplicated, inactive or closed.
- Resume fit measures skills and experience against requirements, and role quality measures relevance, credibility and alignment with goals.
- For students on OPT, hiring timeline and start date can outweigh other positive signals.
- Combined signals sort roles into apply, consider or skip.
- Public data can be incomplete or outdated, so the final decision stays with the student.
