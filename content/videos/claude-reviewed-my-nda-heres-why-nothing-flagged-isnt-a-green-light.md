Someone hears that Claude reviewed their NDA and flagged nothing, and concludes the document is fine to sign. The video says that is the wrong reading. Claude reviews a contract by flagging what is unusual against a standard baseline. It does not give final legal sign-off, and a clean result is not a green light.

## Review is not clearance

The video begins by correcting a word. Claude does not clear an NDA. It reviews it, flagging what stands out against a baseline of standard terms. The right question is not whether Claude can clear the document, but what a review actually catches and what it leaves open.

## A clause quietly missing one thing

The video builds its case on a specific gap. Imagine a confidentiality clause that never carves out information that later becomes public through no fault of anyone, for instance when a competitor publishes it first. That clause reads like a complete, ordinary definition section, and it sits right next to other clauses that do include the standard carve-outs.

A review that only catches obvious red flags can miss a gap like this, because what is missing leaves nothing on the page to stand out.

## The three-part structure

The video describes an NDA review that works as three parts:

- The clauses go in.
- They are checked against a baseline of standard terms, covering definitions, carve-outs, duration, and mutuality.
- A flag list of what is unusual comes out.

## Flagged, then checked

In the video's example, the same confidentiality clause comes back marked in the margin. The note says it is flagged, with no carve-out for public information. That is not confirmation of a problem. It is a prompt to look closer.

The next step is that someone who knows the law that actually applies checks the flagged clause. What counts as a reasonable carve-out or duration differs by state and by deal. Only after that check does the clause move from flagged to either fine after all or something that needs to change.

## Two limits in both directions

The video states the limits plainly. A review only catches gaps measured against its baseline, so a clause outside that baseline can slip through clean. And a heavily flagged NDA is not automatically a bad deal. In both cases, flagged means check this, not reject this.

## Key takeaways

- Claude reviews an NDA and flags unusual items. It does not clear it as a final legal sign-off.
